## Mechanism represented by the physics

Two wheel motors balance and drive. One RS-550 drives a common shaft through an **assumed 25:1 reduction**. Each arm connects to that shaft through its own opposed spring pair. The arms can therefore move differently over bumps without independent height motors.

For each arm, a proposed constant-radius rack coupling gives:

```
x = 0.030 m × (shaft_angle − arm_angle)
spring compression = 0.010 m ± x
branch torque = 2 × 5500 N/m × (0.030 m)² × (shaft_angle − arm_angle)
```

This is 9.9 Nm/rad per branch. The 10 mm preload keeps both compression springs seated in the nominal rollout. A guided rack and arm-side sector gear must realize this relationship; their manufacturing detail is **not complete**. The illustrated coils use an approximate winding count, not manufacturer winding geometry.

The motor briefly increases shaft torque. The springs load and transmit force to the arms, which push the wheels into the ground. The body accelerates upward. After contact is lost, the drive retracts; landing compresses the suspension and the wheel controller recovers balance. There is no release latch or linear screw actuator in this model.

A 25:1 reduction, 80% transmission efficiency, rotor inertia of 1e-5 kg·m², 11.1 V supply and a 45 A current limit are **assumptions**, not selected/measured gearbox, driver or battery specifications. Reverse torque is derated to 75% as an explicit assumption; it does not constitute a validated reverse motor curve.

## Nominal result and limits

Reproduce:

```sh
.venv-sim/bin/python design/concept03/trial.py --save
.venv-sim/bin/python design/concept03/trial.py --no-jump
```

The plant has a free-floating chassis, gravity, ground/tire contacts, independent passive arm joints and three motor actuators. The controller never edits the root pose or velocity after initialization. Tendons do not impose artificial travel-stop impulses. A run leaving the compression-only spring domain fails validation.

Nominal 2.0 kg, level-ground run, 45 ms launch pulse:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Both-wheel clearance, first flight | 52.2 mm |
| First flight duration | 150.5 ms |
| COM rise **after takeoff** | 16.6 mm |
| Spring compression range | 1.20–18.80 mm |
| Maximum body pitch | 2.55° |
| Body-ground contact | None |
| Peak jump motor current | 45 A |
| Maximum wheel torque | 0.393 Nm |
| Final pitch / forward speed at 5 s | 1.63° / 0.031 m/s |
| Approximate horizontal displacement | −102 mm |
| Jump motor copper loss over 5 s | 162 J |

**Wheel clearance is not COM jump height or proven obstacle-clearing ability.** This hop includes leg retraction. Landing rebounds occur; total airborne time across bounces is not the first flight duration. The model does not demonstrate forward obstacle traversal.

`output/report.json` contains explicit pass checks. `output/sensitivity.json` retains the one-at-a-time perturbation results, including failures. For example, 2.2 kg causes body contact; 65% reduction efficiency reduces clearance to about 31 mm; −10% spring rate violates the conservative spring margins. These are development findings, not a robust sim2real qualification.

## Work remaining before hardware release

- Detail and check reduction gears, supports, rack/sector interfaces, fasteners, spring guides and mechanical stops. Imported wheel motor geometry is available but not fitted into final arms. Concept 02's old cylindrical motor mounts do not fit these L-shaped motors.
- Finish a fitted enclosure and support structure. The simulation uses simplified collision geometry; it does not prove all actual hardware clears the enclosure or ground. The concept 02 CAD remains a separate appearance study.
- Close the mass budget using measured parts and printed geometry; 2.0 kg is currently assumed. Inertias, damping, friction, gearbox backlash and tire behavior need measurements.
- Address holding losses and motor heating. The direct, backdrivable common drive draws substantial standing current; a counterbalance or different transmission may be necessary for useful battery life. Repeated jumping is not thermally qualified.
- Select suitable current-controlled drivers, battery, conductors and fusing for measured peak demand. A 45 A software limit is not evidence that an inexpensive driver can sustain it.
- Add encoder/IMU estimation, timing delay, backlash, sensor noise and a realizable controller interface. The feasibility controller currently uses perfect simulated COM/state information, not a deployable controller or trained policy.
- Verify asymmetry, uneven ground, traction and repeat jumps. This nominal test begins symmetrically on level ground; it does not establish outdoor robustness.
- Release printable CAD only after fitting and structural checks. No claim of an unbreakable printed robot, waterproof enclosure or final working hardware is made.

## Independent powered legs option

Replacing the shared drive with one motor/reduction per leg gives **four motors total**: two wheels, two height/jump drives. Both passive spring pairs can remain, so no additional springs are inherently required. It enables commanded left/right height difference, body roll control, load redistribution and independent landing commands. Pitch balance still depends strongly on the wheel motors.

Using the same motor adds $12.50 to the motor/spring subtotal, making **$54.10** before the additional electronics and transmission. It also adds at least the 255 g motor itself. The complete mass and part-count increase depends on the final transmission. Smaller per-leg motors might reduce these penalties, but require a new selection and dynamics check; halving nominal static torque is not enough to size a motor for one-wheel landings.

Budget approximately one extra motor, one extra driver channel, one extra driven-joint sensing channel, another reduction train, extra shaft/bearing support and additional mounting geometry. These are design estimates, not a completed manufacturing BOM. Independent legs improve the available control authority; the benefit depends on developing and validating the corresponding controller. [Ascento's primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2005.11435v1) describes independent hip actuation and an experimental leg-angle-based inward lean mode.
